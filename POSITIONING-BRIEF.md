## 3. Services Framing — Translate to Trades Outcomes

The current services page lists "Web Design, SEO, GBP, Social Media." That's *what we do.* Trades owners care about *what they get.* Reframe each.

### Web Design → "A Website That Books Jobs"
> Mobile-first, fast-loading sites that turn "just looking" into a booked job. Click-to-call buttons everywhere. Quote forms that go straight to your phone. Photo galleries of real work. Built so a homeowner with a flooded basement can call you in three taps.

### SEO → "Get Found When the Phone Stops Ringing"
> You don't need to rank for "best plumber in America." You need to rank for "water heater repair [your city]" — when someone needs you right now. We build the local SEO that puts you on page 1 for the searches that turn into calls.

### Google Business Profile → "The Map Listing That Wins the Job"
> Most of your calls come from Google Maps, not your website. We claim, fill out, and rank your Google Business Profile — photos, services, hours, areas served, response templates — so when someone searches "[trade] near me," yours is the listing they tap.

### Social Media → "Proof You're the Real Deal"
> Customers check your Facebook before they call you. We set up a clean, professional page that shows your work, your team, and your reviews — so the homeowner sees a real local business, not a stranger off the internet.

**Voice rules applied:**
- Outcomes, not features
- Concrete examples ("flooded basement," "water heater repair")
- Plain English, zero jargon
- Friend lead, Rebel undertone ("you don't need to rank for 'best plumber in America'")

## 4. Target Keywords

Two distinct keyword strategies — one for *Lantech's own SEO* (long-tail content play, slow burn), one for *what Lantech delivers to clients* (local pack, the actual product).

### A. Lantech's own SEO — long-tail content (your strategy)

Build blog posts and landing pages around these. Don't expect to rank for 12–18 months. Goal: topical authority + organic leads from owners researching their problem.

**Niche × Service × Area long-tails (Tier 1):**

- "HVAC website design [city]"
- "website for home services business"
- "GBP optimization for plumbers"
- "local SEO for HVAC contractors"
- "how to get more plumbing leads online"
- "website for emergency plumber"
- "Google Business Profile optimization for trades"

**Problem-driven long-tails (Tier 2):**
- "why isn't my plumbing website getting calls"
- "how plumbers rank on Google Maps"
- "best website for HVAC small business"
- "do plumbers need a website"
- "cheap website for electricians"

**Brand-defensive (Tier 3 — easy wins):**
- "Lantech web design"
- "Lantech reviews"
- "[city] web design agency for trades"

**Reserved for later (head terms — don't chase yet):**
- "home services web design"
- "plumber web design"
- "HVAC website company"

### B. Client SEO — local pack (the product Lantech sells)

These are the keywords Lantech RANKS CLIENTS FOR — different battle, fully winnable for a new site with good GMB + reviews + on-page basics:

- "[trade] near me" (e.g., "plumber near me")
- "[city] [trade]" (e.g., "Austin plumber")
- "emergency [trade] [city]" (e.g., "emergency HVAC Phoenix")
- "[specific service] [city]" (e.g., "water heater installation Denver")
- "24 hour [trade] [city]"

This is what makes Lantech credible as an SEO seller — these terms are competitive but winnable, especially in mid-size metros.

**Why this matters for the website copy:**
On services.html and pricing.html, name *real client keywords* in examples ("water heater repair [city]," "HVAC repair [city]"). It signals you understand the buyer's actual SERP, not just SEO theory. Use generic city placeholders or varied US cities — don't lock to SWFL.
