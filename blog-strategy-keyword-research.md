# Blog Content Strategy: Keyword Research Process
**Lantech Web Agency — Content Marketing Documentation**
**Date:** April 2026 | **Author:** Luis Echarri

---

## The Problem We Solved

Lantech's first three blog posts were written without keyword validation. When we audited them, we found the same issue affecting most agency blogs: the topics were written from an agency's perspective ("here's what I know") rather than from a search demand perspective ("here's what my ideal client is actually Googling").

Post 3 — "5 Easy Ways to Boost Your Small Business Website Conversions" — had a double problem: the keyword "small business website conversion tips" is dominated by HubSpot, Unbounce, and Semrush, AND the searchers for that query are SaaS marketers, not small business owners hiring agencies. Wrong competition, wrong audience.

This document covers the research process we now run before writing any blog post.

---

## Our Keyword Validation Framework

Before writing any article, we answer four questions:

### 1. Who is actually searching this?

The target client for Lantech is a small local business owner in the US — a plumber, electrician, HVAC company, landscaper, or restaurant owner who needs a website or local SEO help. They search differently than a marketing manager at a SaaS company.

**Red flag signals:**
- Top 5 results are all SaaS brand blogs (HubSpot, Semrush, Unbounce, NerdWallet)
- Articles discuss "marketing funnels," "MQL," or "B2B pipeline" — not local service businesses
- Question format in SERP is "how do I improve my app's conversion rate" not "why isn't my website getting calls"

**Green flag signals:**
- Results include "near me" modifiers or local business examples
- Searchers are asking from a small operator's perspective ("my plumbing website," "my local bakery")
- SERP shows local agency blogs, freelancer sites, or Quora threads — not major media

### 2. Who ranks on page 1? Can we compete?

A new domain (Lantech launched in 2026) cannot outrank HubSpot for broad informational keywords. But we CAN outrank:
- Small agency blogs with thin content
- Quora threads with scattered answers
- Generic "tips" articles with no local-business specificity
- Sites that rank despite poor E-E-A-T signals

**How we assess competition:**
- Search the keyword in Google Incognito
- Note the domain authority of page 1 results (estimated from brand recognition)
- Note content quality: does page 1 have a dedicated expert, real examples, first-hand data?
- If 3+ results are from major brands (DA 80+), the keyword is too competitive for us now

### 3. What's the search volume?

For a new site, we target the 500–5,000 monthly search range. Here's why:
- Under 500/mo: too niche to build traffic momentum
- 500–5,000/mo: winnable, meaningful traffic if we rank top 3
- Over 5,000/mo: usually too competitive for a new domain without significant domain authority

**How we estimate volume without paid tools:**
- Google Keyword Planner (free): shows broad ranges
- Google Autocomplete depth: more suggestions = more search volume
- "People Also Ask" count: more PAA boxes = Google sees high interest in the topic
- SERP ads count: advertisers pay for high-volume keywords; their presence signals volume

### 4. Can we frame the angle for our audience?

Even a technically mismatched topic can be rescued with the right framing. "Small business website conversion tips" is dominated by SaaS content — but "why isn't my small business website getting calls" speaks directly to a local business owner's lived experience.

**The reframing test:**
- Does our version answer a question a local business owner would type?
- Does our version assume no marketing knowledge?
- Does our version position Lantech as the expert who solves this?

## The Recommendation: 3 New Validated Topics

### Topic A: Website Cost
**Title:** How Much Does a Small Business Website Cost in 2026? (Real Agency Pricing)

| Signal | Data |
|--------|------|
| Primary keyword | "small business website cost 2026" |
| Volume estimate | ~3,500/mo |
| Who ranks page 1 | WebFX, Clutch, small agencies — **winnable** |
| Searcher intent | Commercial/transactional — owner ready to buy |
| Audience match | ✅ Perfect — small business owner pricing research |

**Why this wins:** The intent is pure purchase research. Someone searching this is close to hiring an agency. Lantech can give a real, transparent answer that builds trust immediately. Generic "it depends" content currently dominates — a direct, honest pricing breakdown will stand out.

## The Content Standards We Set

These rules apply to every Lantech blog post going forward:

1. **Validate before writing.** Run the four-question framework. No exceptions.
2. **Lead with the reader's problem.** Hook → Problem → Promise in the intro. No "In this article, we will cover..."
3. **No AI template patterns.** No Key Takeaways boxes, no numbered tip badges, no citation capsules, no gradient text on headings.
4. **Stats cited inline.** "According to Portent (2022), a 1-second site converts 2.5x more than a 5-second site." Not in a box.
5. **First-person voice, once per section.** "I've seen this with clients..." or "We audited 12 businesses and found..." This is E-E-A-T in practice.
6. **Max 1 SVG chart per post.** Used only when a number is genuinely better visualized than stated.
7. **CTR-optimized title tags.** Year, power word, or question format. Under 62 characters.
8. **FAQPage schema on every post.** 4 questions, 40–60 word answers written in plain language.
