# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

US small business owners — plumbers, restaurant owners, salon operators, contractors, dentists, and similar trades. Typically non-technical: they know their trade, not the web. Budget-conscious and often skeptical, having been burned by agencies that overcharged, underdelivered, or handed them a template and disappeared. They judge on feel, clarity, and price — not on spec sheets. They visit the site asking one question: "Can I trust these people with my business?"

The team is PH-based, serving clients fully remotely. Clients are US-only. Copy should speak directly to US small business owners without pretending to be geographically local.

## Product Purpose

Lantech is a web design agency that gives US small businesses a professional online presence at honest, transparent pricing — without the corporate agency overhead and without the AI-template factory aesthetic. It exists as the alternative to two bad options: overpriced full-service agencies and cheap DIY builders that look unprofessional.

Success on any page = visitor books a discovery call or submits the contact form. Every design decision should reduce friction toward that action.

## Brand Personality

**Primary archetype: Friend.** Warm, approachable, on the client's side. Speaks plainly. Takes the client's business seriously without being formal. Never condescending, never jargon-heavy.

**Secondary archetype: Rebel.** Quietly angry at agency overcharging and the AI-template factory model. Takes a clear stance — "Built for small businesses. Not enterprise." — without aggression or irony. The brand's rebellion is expressed through what it refuses to look like, not through noise.

**Three-word personality:** Honest. Warm. Capable.

**Tagline (protected):** "Built for small businesses. Not enterprise." — appears on every page, never modified.

## Anti-references

The specific enemy is "looks like AI made it" — the visual language of AI-generated template sites:
- Identical card grids with icon + heading + text, repeated endlessly
- Purple/cyan gradient hero sections with floating blob backgrounds
- Glassmorphism cards and glass-border effects
- Dark neon SaaS aesthetics (the old Lantech site is itself an anti-reference)
- Generic Webflow or Squarespace agency templates — polished but soulless, indistinguishable from 1,000 others
- Corporate agency sites that signal distance and overhead ("Enterprise solutions for forward-thinking brands")

If a visitor could say "AI made that" without pause, the design has failed.

## Design Principles

1. **Practice what you preach.** The site is the portfolio. If it looks like a template, the pitch fails before a word is read. Every design choice is a proof of capability.

2. **The client's enemy is visible.** The design actively communicates "not corporate, not AI, not expensive" at every touchpoint — through warmth, specificity, and restraint rather than decoration.

3. **Earn trust before asking for action.** Social proof, plain copy, and transparency come before the CTA. The design sequences conviction, not urgency.

4. **One screen, one job.** Every page has one primary action. The layout does not let the visitor wander or get lost in competing priorities.

5. **Authenticity over polish.** Warmth and craft signal more than decorative effects. A site that feels handmade and specific beats one that feels AI-assembled and generic every time.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA compliance as the baseline. No specific known user needs beyond defaults. Support `prefers-reduced-motion` for all non-essential transitions.
