# One-Time Website Build Packages for Home Services Contractors
## US Agency Market Research — May 2026

---

## Executive Summary

This report analyzes what US web agencies charge for one-time, flat-rate website builds targeting home services contractors — plumbers, HVAC technicians, electricians, and roofers.

**The headline finding:** The majority of niche home services agencies do not offer a true one-time flat-rate build. Most bundle the website into a monthly retainer (Built-Right Digital, Hook Agency, Rival Digital, Scorpion) or charge a setup fee alongside a mandatory monthly subscription (Online Access Inc., Home Pro Digital). Only a small number of agencies offer a clean, pay-once-own-everything model — and those that do publish prices are primarily productized or general-contractor-focused firms.

**Where flat-rate one-time builds do exist:**
- The market consensus for 5-page builds: **$1,499–$2,500**
- For 10-page builds with service-area pages: **$2,499–$4,000**
- For 15–20-page multi-city builds: **$4,000–$8,000**

**Lantech's current pricing ($1,200 / $1,699 / $1,999) sits at the bottom of these ranges** — competitive at the Starter tier but underpriced at Growth and Pro relative to comparable market offerings.

**The most important structural finding:** Emergency service pages, city/service-area pages, and Google Business Profile optimization are considered mid-to-premium features in the one-time build market. Agencies that offer these at the entry tier typically charge $2,500+.

## Introduction

### Scope

This research covers US web agencies that serve home services contractors (plumbers, HVAC, electricians, roofers) and offer one-time flat-rate or project-based website build packages. Monthly retainer/SEO packages are included only where they illuminate how the market structures and prices standalone builds.

### Methodology

Seven agencies with published pricing were identified and their pricing pages fetched directly. Twelve additional agencies without published pricing were confirmed as using custom-quote or subscription models. Editorial pricing guides from Built-Right Digital, Nopio, and CMMM Studios — which publish detailed industry price breakdowns — were used to establish market consensus ranges.

### Key Assumptions

- "Flat-rate" means a defined price for a defined scope, paid once, with the client owning all files.
- Agencies that charge a setup fee alongside a mandatory 24-month monthly commitment are classified as **subscription with setup**, not flat-rate.
- Pricing data reflects published rates as of May 2026. Many agencies require a quote — their pricing is not included in the tier tables.

## Finding 1: Most Home Services Agencies Don't Offer True Flat-Rate Builds

The most important finding from this research is structural: **dedicated home services web agencies have moved away from one-time project pricing.** The dominant model in this niche is one of three alternatives:

**Model A — Full retainer (site included):** The agency builds and hosts the site as part of an ongoing monthly engagement. The client never "owns" the files outright — access ends if they leave. Used by: Built-Right Digital ($1,199–$19,999/mo), Rival Digital, Scorpion, Hook Agency ($1,750/mo web design add-on), Online Access Inc. ($659–$3,659/mo).

**Model B — Setup fee + mandatory monthly:** The agency charges a one-time fee to build the site, but the client must stay on a monthly subscription for hosting, maintenance, and support — usually with a 12–24 month minimum. Used by: Home Pro Digital (LAUNCH: $500 setup / BUILD: $1,000 setup / DOMINATE: $1,500 setup — all require 24-month commitment), Online Access Inc. ($995 startup + monthly).

**Model C — True flat-rate one-time:** Client pays once, gets all files, owns everything. Monthly hosting/maintenance is optional or sold separately. Used by: ContractorWeb.net, CMMM Studios, and general contractor agencies like Drive Web Design (though the latter blends setup with ongoing).

**Why niche agencies moved away from flat-rate builds:** Retainer models generate predictable recurring revenue and keep clients from taking the built site to a cheaper host. Agencies also argue that a website without ongoing SEO produces few leads — so the build alone isn't a viable product. The market has largely validated this reasoning.

**What this means for Lantech:** A true flat-rate one-time model is a genuine market gap in the home services niche. Only 2–3 agencies clearly offer it with published prices. Lantech's "build once, own everything" positioning is a real differentiator — but it also means there is limited direct pricing competition to benchmark against.

## Finding 3: Market Consensus on What Each Price Tier Delivers

Across all sources — including Built-Right Digital's editorial pricing guides for HVAC, roofing, plumbing, and electrician websites — a clear market consensus emerges on what contractors should expect at each price point.

### Budget Tier: $500–$2,500

**Who builds at this price:** Productized agencies (CMMM Studios), general freelancers, template agencies.

**What's typically included:**
- 3–5 pages (Home, About, Services, Contact, optionally a 5th specialty page)
- Mobile-responsive template design (not custom)
- Click-to-call buttons on every page
- Basic contact form
- On-page SEO: meta tags, title tags, sitemap
- Google Business Profile setup (not optimization)
- Stock photography
- SSL and basic hosting for 1 year (sometimes)

**What is NOT included at this tier:**
- City/service-area pages (these require individual page builds)
- Emergency service page
- Custom design or copywriting
- Google Analytics + Search Console setup (often omitted)
- Schema markup beyond basic LocalBusiness
- Review integration
- Call tracking

**Market examples:** CMMM Studios Starter ($497), CMMM Studios Business ($997), ContractorWeb Professional ($1,499), Drive Web Design Basic ($1,000–$2,000 setup)

---

### Mid Tier: $2,500–$5,000

**Who builds at this price:** Specialized contractor agencies, custom-design freelancers, boutique local firms.

**What's typically included:**
- 8–15 pages
- Dedicated service pages (one per major service: drain cleaning, water heater repair, AC repair, etc.)
- 2–5 city/service-area pages for top target cities
- Custom design (not template)
- Professional copywriting included or guided
- Google Business Profile optimization (not just setup)
- Photo gallery (real job photos or optimized stock)
- Social proof integration (Google Reviews widget)
- On-page SEO + basic off-page (citation submissions)
- Google Analytics + Search Console setup
- Schema markup (LocalBusiness + Service)
- Mobile optimization to Core Web Vitals standards
- Contact form + quote form

**What is NOT included at this tier:**
- Emergency service page (usually a premium add-on or retainer feature)
- Call tracking (typically starts at retainer tier)
- Ongoing monthly updates
- Blog structure
- Competitor analysis

**Market examples:** ContractorWeb Premium ($2,499, 10 pages), Drive Web Design Professional ($2,000–$4,000 setup, 8–12 pages), CMMM Studios Premium ($2,497+), Built-Right editorial mid-market ($3,000–$6,000 for roofing)

---

### Premium Tier: $5,000–$15,000

**Who builds at this price:** Established contractor-niche agencies, full-service digital agencies.

**What's typically included:**
- 15–25+ pages
- Full service area page architecture (every city served gets its own page)
- Emergency service / "24-hour near me" page
- Custom design from scratch
- Professional copywriting on every page
- GBP fully optimized + initial post strategy
- 150+ citation directory submissions
- Full schema stack (LocalBusiness + Service + FAQPage)
- Call tracking setup
- Blog structure with 2–4 initial posts
- Competitor analysis
- Core Web Vitals passing score guaranteed
- 30–90 day post-launch support window

**Market examples:** Drive Web Design Lead Gen Machine ($3,500–$5,000+ setup), Built-Right editorial premium ($3,000–$8,000 roofing, $4,000–$8,000 electrician, $5,000–$12,000 HVAC/plumber), general agency custom ($5,000–$15,000)

## Finding 5: Pricing by Trade — Does the Trade Matter?

Market sources distinguish pricing by trade in their editorial content, but the differentiators are complexity-driven, not trade-specific. The same tier framework applies across plumbing, HVAC, electrical, and roofing. What varies by trade:

- **HVAC and plumbing** tend to require more service pages (more distinct services: AC repair, AC installation, furnace repair, furnace installation, ductwork, etc.), pushing mid-tier builds toward 12–15 pages.
- **Roofing** often requires a simpler page structure but heavier before/after photo galleries.
- **Electrical** tends toward more city-page depth given the broad geographic coverage of many electrical contractors.

In practice, agencies serving these trades price identically across trades at each tier. The trade-specific pricing on Built-Right Digital's site ($1,500–$4,500 for plumbing, $1,500–$3,000 basic for electrical, $500–$2,500 bootstrap for roofing) reflects the same underlying tier structure — just described from each trade's perspective.

## Finding 6: Where Lantech's Current Pricing Sits

This analysis directly addresses Lantech's current one-time build pricing ($1,200 / $1,699 / $1,999) against market data.

| Tier | Lantech Price | Pages | Market Comparable | Market Price | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | $1,200 | 5 | ContractorWeb Professional | $1,499 (5 pages) | **Competitive** — slightly under ContractorWeb's 5-page equivalent; fair entry price |
| **Growth** | $1,699 | 10 | ContractorWeb Premium | $2,499 (10 pages) | **Underpriced** — $800 below market for 10 pages; sustainable only if service area pages are 2–3, not full city coverage |
| **Pro** | $1,999 | 20 | Drive Web Design Professional | $2,000–$4,000 setup | **Significantly underpriced** — 20 pages with emergency page and competitor analysis at $1,999 is well below the $4,000–$8,000 market rate for this scope |

**Key implications:**

1. **The Pro tier ($1,999, 20 pages) is the biggest concern.** Twenty pages with emergency-service messaging, multi-city service-area pages, Facebook Business Page setup, and competitor analysis is a premium-tier build. The market charges $4,000–$8,000 for this scope. At $1,999, Lantech is either underselling significantly or the 20-page count includes very lightweight pages.

2. **The Growth tier ($1,699, 10 pages)** is below ContractorWeb's closest comparable ($2,499 for 10 pages). If Lantech includes service-area pages for top cities in Growth, that's good value — but the price still needs to support the labor involved.

3. **The Starter ($1,200, 5 pages)** is the most defensible price point. It's slightly below the ContractorWeb comparable and squarely in the market range for a 5-page template-based build with basic SEO.

## Synthesis: Three Structural Observations

**1. Flat-rate one-time is a real gap in the home services niche.**

Because most agencies have moved to retainer-first or subscription models, a contractor who wants a one-time build — pay once, own everything, no contracts — has very few credible agency options. Lantech's positioning here is genuinely differentiated. The key is pricing it sustainably.

**2. Service-area/city pages are the primary mid-tier differentiator.**

Every source agrees: city-specific service-area pages are what move a website from "brochure" to "lead generator." They're the feature that jumps a build from the budget tier to the mid tier. Including even 3 city pages in a $1,699 package is a stronger value proposition than 10 generic pages.

**3. Emergency pages are a premium feature in one-time builds — but essential in retainers.**

In the one-time build market, emergency service pages ("24/7 [Trade] Near Me") are typically a $4,000+ feature. In retainer plans, they're included because the agency builds and manages all pages ongoing. This explains — and validates — the inconsistency the user identified: having emergency pages only in the Pro one-time tier ($1,999) while including them in all retainer tiers is actually market-standard behavior. The fix isn't to remove them from retainers — it's to recognize the one-time Pro tier may need a price increase to justify what it delivers.

## Recommendations for Lantech

Based on this research, four adjustments to the one-time build tiers are supported by market data:

**1. Raise the Pro tier price to $2,799–$3,200.**
Twenty pages with service-area architecture, emergency page, Facebook Business Page, and competitor analysis is a $4,000–$8,000 build in the market. Even at $2,999, Lantech undercuts ContractorWeb's 10-page package ($2,499) while delivering twice the pages. At $1,999, the math doesn't hold unless builds are heavily templated.

**2. Keep the Starter tier at $1,200 or raise to $1,299.**
This is the most competitive price point and serves as a genuine entry-level alternative to Wix or a freelancer. It's defensible as-is.

**3. Raise Growth to $1,999–$2,299, or clarify what "10 pages" means.**
If Growth includes 3–5 service-area city pages (which it should to be a lead generator), it's worth $2,499 based on ContractorWeb's comparable. If it's 10 generic pages with no city coverage, $1,699 is reasonable but the offer is weaker.

**4. Resolve the emergency-page inconsistency between tiers.**
The most logical fix: add emergency page to Growth as well (not Starter), and price Growth accordingly. Emergency page is the #1 thing that makes a website generate after-hours calls — positioning it as Pro-only in the one-time build tier is a missed selling point at the Growth tier.

## Limitations and Caveats

- Most niche home services agencies do not publish pricing publicly. This research is based on the minority that do, plus industry editorial guides. Actual agency prices may differ by geography, client size, and project complexity.
- CMMM Studios and ContractorWeb.net are the only direct flat-rate comparables found with published pricing. Both serve general contractors, not exclusively plumbers/HVAC.
- Prices cited are from May 2026 and reflect published rates. Actual negotiated rates may be lower.
- Drive Web Design pricing is for the South Jersey/Philadelphia market; prices may not reflect national rates.
