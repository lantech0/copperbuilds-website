How We Work

This is CopperBuilds's complete process guide — every step from finding a new client all the way to offboarding them. Written so anyone on the team can pick up and do the work correctly, even without prior experience.

Finding Leads

We find small businesses that need help with their online presence and reach out with a specific, personal message — not a generic pitch.

What we're looking for

Target businesses in home services (plumbers, HVAC, electricians, roofers, landscapers, etc.) that have one or more of these problems:

A
No website at all — highest priority, easiest sell
B
Has a website but it's outdated, not mobile-friendly, or loads slowly
C
No Facebook or Instagram page, or their social accounts are completely inactive
D
Good Google reviews but no online presence to capture the demand

How to find them

1
Search Google Maps for the trade and city
Example: "plumbers in Tampa FL." Go through every result and note their website status, GBP completeness, and review count.
2
Find the "Gold Standard" competitor first
Before reaching out to anyone, find the best-ranked business in that trade and city. This is the business you'll reference in every outreach message — "Your competitor [Name] is pulling in all the searches you're missing."
3
Score each lead
No website = 40 pts. No Facebook = 20 pts. No Instagram = 15 pts. High rating with low visibility = 5 pts. Hot leads (70+ pts) get outreach first.
4
Find the owner's name
Search Google, Facebook, or LinkedIn for the owner's first name. "Hi John" converts much better than "Hi Team." Mark ❌ if you can't find it after 2–3 searches — don't guess.
5
Write 3 outreach messages per hot/warm lead
One email, one Facebook/Instagram DM, one LinkedIn message. Each one must mention the Gold Standard competitor by name and include the revenue opportunity number. Never send a generic message.
6
Save everything to the prospect file
All leads, scores, contact info, and outreach messages go into one file in projects/prospects/. Never leave leads scattered in notes or memory.
⚠
Before starting any sessionCheck the existing prospect file for this trade + city. Do not research or contact any business that's already on the list. Duplicates waste time and damage our credibility if we reach out twice.

The Discovery Call

A 20-minute structured call that qualifies the prospect, surfaces their real problem, and closes them on a CopperBuilds plan — or identifies why they're not a fit right now.

Before the call (5 minutes of prep)

1
Pull up the prospect's entry in projects/prospects/ — review their score, gaps, and revenue opportunity figure.
2
Open their website (if any) and Google Business Profile. Know specifically what's wrong before the call starts.
3
Decide which plan you're recommending before the call. Do not figure it out on the call — it makes you look unprepared.

Call structure (20 minutes)

1
Opening — 2 minutes
Introduce yourself, set the agenda: "I wanted to spend about 20 minutes learning about your business and showing you what we found. Sound good?"
2
Discovery — 8 minutes
Ask these 4 questions and listen more than you talk:

"How are customers finding you right now?"
"Have you tried any marketing before? How did that go?"
"If you had 5 new customers a month from Google, what would that mean for your business?"
"What's holding you back from having a proper web presence right now?"
3
The pitch — 6 minutes
Lead with the specific gap you found. Name the Gold Standard competitor and the revenue they're capturing. Then introduce the solution and present one plan — not all three. Show one portfolio demo that matches their trade. One direction only — showing options creates doubt.
4
Handle objections — 2 minutes
See the objection guide below. Stay calm — objections mean they're thinking about it, not that they're saying no.
5
Close — 2 minutes
If they're ready: "I'll send the agreement and invoice today. Once signed and paid, you'll get the onboarding questionnaire and we start building within 24 hours."

If they want to think: book a specific follow-up time before hanging up. Send a follow-up email within 1 hour.

Handling common objections

What they sayWhat you say
"Too expensive""Compare it to what [competitor] is making off those [N,NNN] monthly searches — $[price] is one or two jobs you're already losing every month."
"I'll think about it""Totally fair — what specifically do you want to think over? I want to make sure you have the right information." Then book a follow-up before hanging up.
"I'll do it myself""Most of our clients tried that first. The issue isn't building the site — it's getting it to rank on Google. That's where we add the real value."
"I don't trust online agencies""That's exactly why we're month-to-month — no annual contracts, cancel anytime with 30 days notice. You own all your files and can move them anywhere."
"I already have a website""I took a look at it. [Specific finding — e.g., 'It's not loading on mobile' / 'It loads in 6 seconds']. That's costing you Google rankings right now."

After the call

✓
If they said yes
Send the service agreement and invoice the same day. Run the Client Intake process (Section 4).
✓
If they need time
Send a follow-up email within 1 hour. Note their specific objection. Set a calendar reminder for the follow-up date you agreed on.
✓
If they're not a fit
Mark the lead as Dead with a reason note. No further contact.

Sending a Proposal

When a prospect asks for something in writing before deciding — we send a short, specific, one-page proposal within 24 hours of the call. Speed matters here. The faster the follow-up, the higher the close rate.

⚠
Decide the plan before you write anythingThe proposal recommends one specific plan — not a menu of options. Three options create indecision. Pick the right one based on the prospect's city count and budget signals from the call.
1
Pull the prospect file
Open clients/prospects/[file].md and confirm you have: the business name and owner name, the top 2–3 specific gaps you found, the revenue opportunity figure, and the recommended plan and price.
2
Write the proposal
Maximum 2 pages. No agency jargon. Written to the business owner, not a boardroom. Include: what's wrong right now (specific), what we'll build, what it costs, what happens next, and the delivery timeline. They should be able to read it in 2 minutes and know exactly what they're getting.
3
Save the proposal
Save as clients/prospects/[slug]-proposal-YYYY-MM-DD.md. Update the prospect file status to "Proposal Sent — [date]".
4
Send the email
Subject: "Your CopperBuilds Proposal — [Business Name]." Keep the email body under 6 lines. Paste the proposal inline or attach as PDF. Same day is ideal. Next morning is acceptable. Beyond 24 hours, momentum is gone.
5
Set follow-up reminders
If no reply in 3 business days: check in. If still no reply at 7 business days: one final follow-up. After 14 days of silence: mark as cold. No further contact unless they reach out.
6
When they say yes
Send the service agreement and invoice the same day. Run Client Intake (Section 4). Update the prospect file to "Closed — [date]".

Client Intake

The moment a client says yes, we move fast. Every new client gets a folder, a service agreement, an invoice, and an onboarding questionnaire — all sent within 24 hours of closing.

1
Create the client folder
Inside clients/active/, create a folder named [client-slug]/ (e.g., keiths-roofing/). Copy all template files from clients/templates/ into the folder. Fill in the business name, contact info, and plan details in client.env.
2
Send the service agreement
Use the template in clients/templates/03-service-agreement.md. Fill in the client's name, business name, plan name, price, and start date. Send via email with subject: "CopperBuilds Service Agreement — [Business Name]."
3
Send the invoice
Send the first invoice immediately. For monthly plans: invoice the first month now, then the same date every month. For one-time website builds only: 50% deposit upfront, 50% on final approval — state this in the agreement before signing.
4

Send clients/templates/02-onboarding-questionnaire.md. This collects logo files, photos, service list, service areas, hours, phone number, and any existing assets. Tell the client: "The faster you get this back to me, the faster we start building."

Open clients/templates/12-onboarding-checklist.md and go through every item. Do not start building until every required input is collected — a half-complete questionnaire always leads to a half-built site.
6

Do not start building until the invoice is paid (or the deposit is confirmed). No exceptions. Once payment is confirmed, add the client to the active tracker and set a build start date.
✓
The 24-hour ruleSend the agreement, invoice, and questionnaire within 24 hours of the client saying yes. Momentum is highest right after the call. Waiting a day or two often kills deals that were already won.

Building the Website

We build static HTML sites that load fast, rank on Google, and look professional on every device. Every build follows the same quality standards — no exceptions based on plan size.

Before writing a single line of code

1
Run keyword research
Find the exact terms people in the client's city type into Google for their service. Examples: "plumber Tampa FL", "emergency HVAC repair Tampa", "best roofer near me." This determines the page titles, headings, and content. Do this before anything else.
2
Map every page to a keyword
Homepage → primary city + service. Service pages → each service type. City pages → each target city. Write down the target keyword for every page before building it. Never build a page without knowing its keyword first.
3
Set up the project folder
Create the build folder at portfolio/[client-slug]/. All HTML, CSS, images, and assets go here. Do not mix client files with CopperBuilds's own site files.

Every page must pass these checks before the client sees anything

Page Quality Gate
Target keyword is in the <title> tag, the main heading (H1), and the first paragraph
Meta description is written — 150 to 160 characters, one clear sentence, includes the keyword
Every image has an alt text description (example: alt="Tampa plumber fixing kitchen sink")
Phone number is clickable on mobile — tapping it starts a call
Contact form works — fill it out yourself and check that it sends
No broken links — every nav link opens a real page
Loads in under 3 seconds on a regular 4G connection
Looks correct on mobile (375px wide) — no horizontal scrolling, no cut-off text
Business name, phone, and address are spelled exactly as they appear on Google Maps
Schema markup (LocalBusiness JSON-LD) is in the <head> of the homepage

Preview & Revisions

Once the site is built, we show the client a preview, collect feedback, and apply up to 2 rounds of revisions — then get written approval to go live.

1
Send the preview
Share the staging URL or a screenshot walkthrough by email. Subject: "Your CopperBuilds Site Preview — [Business Name]." Give the client 48 hours to review, then follow up if you haven't heard back.
2
Collect all feedback in one batch
Ask the client to send all feedback in a single email — not in pieces over several days. This keeps the scope of Round 1 clear and prevents endless back-and-forth.
3
Apply Round 1 revisions
Fix everything clearly requested: wrong phone numbers, typos, incorrect service names, photo swaps. If a request would change the design direction significantly, confirm with the client before changing. Send updated preview within 48 hours.
4
Apply Round 2 revisions (if needed)
Collect and apply a second round. After Round 2, the site is considered approved. Anything else beyond this point is out of scope and billed hourly.
5
Get written approval before going live
The client must reply by email confirming approval. "Looks great, go ahead" in an email is sufficient. Do not go live based on a phone call — email confirmation only, always.
⚠
2 rounds is the limit — make it clear earlyThe service agreement covers 2 revision rounds. If a client wants more, remind them politely before starting Round 3 so there are no billing surprises. Say: "We've used both revision rounds included in your plan. I can make these additional changes for $X/hour."

Setting Up Analytics

We set up Google Analytics 4 and Google Search Console immediately after go-live. These tools are how we track results and produce monthly reports. Without them, we have no data to show.

1
Use the client's Google account
The client must own their own analytics — never set this up under your personal Google account. Ask for access to their account during onboarding. If they already have GA4 or Search Console, ask for Editor access instead of starting fresh.
2
Set up Google Analytics 4 (GA4)
Go to analytics.google.com → Create property → enter the business name and website URL. Copy the tracking snippet (it looks like "G-XXXXXXXXXX") and paste it into the <head> of every page on the site.
3
Set up Google Search Console (GSC)
Go to search.google.com/search-console → Add property → enter the full site URL. Verify ownership by uploading the HTML verification file Google gives you (place it in the site's root folder on Hostinger), then click Verify.
4
Link GA4 to Search Console
In GA4 → Admin → Property settings → Search Console links → Add. This connects the two tools so you can see which search terms are bringing visitors to the site. Takes 48–72 hours to start showing data.
5
Set up conversion tracking
In GA4 → Configure → Events → mark "form_submit" and "phone_call_click" as Key Events. This tells GA4 what a lead looks like. Without this, monthly reports can only show visitors — not actual enquiries.
6
Add your CopperBuilds account as Editor
In both GA4 and Search Console, add your CopperBuilds Google account as an Editor. This gives you access for monthly reporting without needing the client's login every time.
7
Submit the sitemap
In Search Console → Sitemaps → enter sitemap.xml and submit. This tells Google to crawl all the site's pages. The sitemap file should already be in the site root — if not, create one listing every page URL.
✓
GA4 takes 24–48 hours to show dataThe dashboard will look empty for the first day or two. That's normal. The first monthly report will cover the full first month — not just the first few days.

30-Day Follow-Up

At the 30-day mark, we check in with the client, ask for a testimonial, and offer the right upsell if it makes sense. This is also when the first real performance data starts to appear.

1
Set the reminder on launch day
When the site goes live, set a calendar reminder for exactly 30 days later. Don't rely on memory — if this follow-up slips, the client feels forgotten.
2
Pull the first data
Open GA4 and Search Console. Look for: total visitors in the first 30 days, which search terms the site is appearing for, any form submissions or phone call clicks recorded. Even small numbers are a conversation starter.
3
Send the check-in email
Keep it short. Lead with one positive number. Ask how the phone has been. Ask if there's anything they want to adjust on the site. Example: "Your site showed up in Google Search 420 times in the first 30 days — how has the phone been?"
4
Ask for a Google review
Every client gets one review request. Send the Google review link in the same email. Keep it simple: "If you're happy with the work so far, a quick Google review means the world to us — here's the link." Don't push — one ask only.
5
Offer the upsell if it makes sense
If the client is on Local Presence and the site is performing, mention Lead Machine. If they're on Lead Machine and seeing traction, mention Market Leader. Only offer if it genuinely makes sense — don't upsell just to upsell.
ℹ
You don't need big numbers to make this call worth itEven "Your site has been indexed and is showing up for 12 different searches" is a meaningful update for someone who had no web presence a month ago. The follow-up is about making them feel looked after — not impressing them with traffic reports.

LSA Setup — Market Leader Only

LSA (Google Local Services Ads) are the ads that appear at the very top of Google with a "Google Guaranteed" badge. This setup is a first-month task for every new Market Leader client.

⚠
The client pays Google directly — this is not part of our feeLSA ad spend is charged by Google to the client's credit card — it is NOT included in the $3,497/month CopperBuilds fee. Our fee covers the setup and the monthly management. The actual cost per lead (typically $20–80 for home services) is between the client and Google. Always explain this before setting anything up.

What LSA is (in plain English)

When someone in the client's city searches "plumber near me" or "HVAC repair Tampa," LSA ads appear at the very top of Google — above the regular paid ads, above the map, above everything. Businesses with the green "Google Guaranteed" badge appear here. The client only pays when a verified customer calls through the ad. Google screens out spam calls and wrong numbers — the client can also dispute bad leads inside the dashboard.

How to get the client Google Guaranteed (first month only)

1
Create or access the client's Google account
The Google account used for LSA should be the same one tied to their Google Business Profile. If they don't have one, create it with their business email.
2
Apply at ads.google.com/local-services-ads
Click "Get started" and select "Local Services Ads." Sign in with the client's Google account. Select their business category (e.g., Plumber, HVAC, Roofer, Electrician, Landscaper).
3
Enter the service area and business hours
Set the cities and zip codes where the client works. Set their hours of availability. These control when and where the ads show up.
4
Upload the business license
Google requires proof of a valid business license. Ask the client for a photo or PDF of their current license. Upload it directly in the LSA application portal.
5
Upload proof of insurance
Google also requires a current Certificate of Liability Insurance (COI). Ask the client for this document — their insurance agent can provide it. Upload it in the portal. The minimum coverage requirement varies by trade.
6
Pass the background check
Google runs a background check on the business owner and any employees listed. The client will receive an email from a third-party screener (usually Pinkerton or Evident). Tell the client to watch for this email and complete it within a few days — delays here hold up the whole verification.
7
Set the weekly budget
Help the client set a realistic starting budget. A safe starting point for most home services trades is $200–400/week. They can adjust it any time in the dashboard. Remind them again: this is their money going to Google — separate from the CopperBuilds fee.
8
Wait for Google to approve
After all documents are submitted, Google typically takes 3–10 business days to review and approve. The client will get an email when approved. Once live, the "Google Guaranteed" badge appears on their listing.

Monthly Report

Every client on a retainer gets a monthly report — sent in the last week of the month. One key win at the top, real numbers, and specific plans for next month. No fluff.

1
Pull data from GA4 and Search Console
Log into both tools. Record: total visitors this month vs. last month, top search terms the site appeared for, how many clicks from Google, and how many form submissions or call clicks were tracked. Take note of any significant changes — up or down.
2
Pull GBP insights
In the client's Google Business Profile → Performance. Record: how many people viewed the profile, how many clicked to call, how many asked for directions. This shows local visibility independent of the website.
3
Pull GHL dashboard data
Log into the client's GoHighLevel sub-account. Pull: total inbound calls, missed calls, missed call text-back responses, leads in pipeline, review requests sent vs. responded. Note patterns — busiest call days, pipeline conversion rate, review response rate.
4
Identify the one key win
Find the single most positive result from the month — a traffic increase, a new keyword ranking, a spike in calls, a citation fixed. Lead the report with this. It sets the tone for everything that follows.
5
Write the report
Use the template in clients/active/[slug]/monthly-reports/. Fill every section — no blank fields, no "data pending." If a metric isn't available, explain why briefly. Keep the language plain: "Your site showed up in Google 1,840 times this month" beats "impressions totalled 1,840 across organic channels."
6
List what's planned for next month
End the report with 3–5 specific actions for next month — not vague things like "continue optimizing." Specific: "Write a blog post targeting 'emergency HVAC repair Tampa'", "Submit to 3 new local directories", "Dispute 2 LSA leads from last week."
7
Send by the last day of the month
Email subject: "Your CopperBuilds Monthly Report — [Month] [Year]." Send to the client contact on file. Save a copy to clients/active/[slug]/monthly-reports/YYYY-MM-report.md.
⚠
No blank fields, everA report with empty sections looks worse than no report at all. If you don't have data for a metric, write one sentence explaining why (e.g., "GA4 access was set up mid-month — full data available in next month's report"). Then fix the access issue so it doesn't happen again.

Portfolio Capture

After every client launch, we capture the work for our portfolio. This is how we get more clients — prospective clients look at the portfolio before deciding. Treat it as part of the launch process, not an optional extra.

1
Take screenshots within 48 hours of launch

When a Client Leaves

When a client cancels, we handle it professionally. They own their site, their data, and their accounts — we hand everything over cleanly and part on good terms. A graceful exit often leads to a referral.

1
Confirm the cancellation
When a client says they want to cancel, ask one question to make sure: "Just to confirm — you'd like to end your plan with 30 days notice starting today. Is that right?" Get a yes in writing before doing anything else.
2
Check for any outstanding balance
Confirm all invoices are paid before closing the account. If there's an outstanding balance, address it now — do not hand over files while money is still owed.
3
Package and deliver the site files
Zip all HTML, CSS, image, and asset files for their site. Send the zip file to the client by email. They now own a full copy of everything we built.
4
Transfer Google Analytics and Search Console
In GA4: promote the client to Admin, then remove the CopperBuilds account. In Search Console: add the client as Owner, then remove the CopperBuilds account. Do this in the same email as the file delivery.
5
Transfer hosting access
If the site is hosted on Hostinger under our account: provide the client with FTP credentials and instructions for downloading the files, or initiate a hosting transfer if they want to stay on the same server. If they're moving to their own host, provide an upload-ready zip and basic instructions.
6
Transfer domain access (if applicable)
If the domain is registered under our Hostinger or Namecheap account, initiate a domain transfer to the client's registrar account. Send the transfer authorization code. This takes 5–7 business days.
7
Capture the portfolio entry
Run the Portfolio Capture process (Section 13) if you haven't already. Take screenshots of the live site before any access is removed. The work is done — it belongs in the portfolio regardless of the reason for leaving.
8
Send the goodbye email
Send a warm, professional closing email. Thank them for the time together. Include all the transfer details in one place. Offer to answer any questions. End with: "If you ever need us again, we're here." This email determines whether they refer us to someone else.
9
Archive the client folder
Move clients/active/[slug]/ to clients/completed/[slug]/. Add a final note to the client file with the cancellation date and reason (if known). Do not delete anything.
✓
How you handle the exit matters as much as the workMost clients who leave do so for budget reasons, not because of bad service. A clean, generous handover keeps the door open for when they come back — and they often do.
