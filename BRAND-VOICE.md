# Brand Voice Guidelines
## CopperBuilds
### Analysis Date: 2026-04-29 · Updated 2026-09-14 (post-rebrand: Lantech → CopperBuilds)

## Voice Summary

CopperBuilds' copy speaks like a Friend/Rebel — warm, direct, and on the small business owner's side. The tagline "Built for small businesses. Not enterprise." is genuinely bold and positions against the agency industry. This analysis originally found that the visual identity (dark neon SaaS aesthetics) contradicted the voice; the rebrand to CopperBuilds resolved that — the current warm, editorial, light-mode identity in `DESIGN.md` matches the Friend/Rebel voice this document locks in. The dimension targets and copy guidance below remain the active standard.

---

## Voice Dimensions

### Formal ←——→ Casual: **6/10**
CopperBuilds writes conversationally. Contractions, short sentences, and everyday language are the norm.

Evidence:
- *"We're a dedicated studio with one mission: getting small businesses online with websites that actually work."* — "that actually work" is plain spoken, not corporate.
- *"at a price that makes sense"* — nobody says this in a formal brand voice.
- *"Four services. One focused team."* — punchy, direct, no padding.
- *"without the agency overhead"* — names the enemy (agencies) without euphemism.

### Serious ←——→ Playful: **3/10**
The voice is earnest and purposeful. There is no humor yet. This is acceptable for a trust-building service brand — clients don't want their web agency to be goofy. But there is room to add warmth and the occasional light moment without going full Mailchimp.

Evidence: No puns, no wordplay, no self-deprecation. Every sentence earns its place. This is not a weakness — it matches the Friend archetype's "we take your business seriously" signal.

### Technical ←——→ Simple: **5/10**
Mixed. The service descriptions mention "Core Web Vitals", "schema markup", and "Google Search Console" without explaining them. The hero copy is simple. This gap needs closing — small business owners don't know what Core Web Vitals means and they shouldn't have to.

Evidence:
- Technical: *"Local keyword research, schema markup, Google Search Console integration, and Core Web Vitals optimization."*
- Simple: *"convert visitors into real customers — at a price that makes sense."*

**Target for new voice: 7/10 toward Simple.** Explain every technical term or drop it.

### Reserved ←——→ Bold: **6/10**
The brand takes a clear stance against big agencies, which is bold. But it stops short of full Rebel energy. Claims are mostly safe ("50+ businesses served" is humble, not bold).

Evidence:
- Bold: *"Built for small businesses. Not enterprise."* — this is a values declaration.
- Reserved: *"Professional websites for small businesses"* — the hero headline is generic.

**Target for new voice: 8/10 toward Bold.** The tagline is the brand's best line. The hero headline should match its energy.

## Brand Personality

- **Primary Archetype: The Friend**
  Warm, on your side, makes complex things feel manageable. Talks to small business owners like a knowledgeable ally, not a vendor. Key signal: "We'll take care of it — you focus on your business."

- **Secondary Archetype: The Rebel**
  Motivated by injustice, specifically the way big agencies overcharge and under-deliver for small businesses. The Rebel edge gives the Friend backbone. It's what separates CopperBuilds from "nice but generic" agencies.

- **Combined voice feel:** A trusted local expert who's quietly angry on your behalf. Not performatively aggressive, just honest about the problem and confident about the solution.

- **Archetype fit:** Strong. Every answer in the archetype discovery exercise pointed here. The tagline already embodies it, and the visual identity now matches it too.

**Reference brands with same archetype combination:**
- Mailchimp (Friend/Rebel — warm yellow, plain-spoken, anti-corporate, named the agency problem directly)
- Basecamp (Friend/Rebel — calls out bad agency practices, warm and honest, takes a stand)
- Harvest (Friend — approachable, human-scale, clearly built for small teams not enterprise)

## Vocabulary

### Words We Use

**Action words:**
- build, launch, get found, convert, grow, fix, deliver, handle, take care of

**Descriptive words:**
- affordable, fast, real, honest, clear, focused, simple, mobile-first

**Value words:**
- flat-rate, no contracts, 14 days, small business, local, transparent

Note: never use "US-based" as a value word. CopperBuilds serves home services pros across the US but the team is PH-based; correct phrasing is "serving home services pros across the US," never a claim of being geographically US-based.

**Phrases that feel like us:**
- "websites that actually work"
- "at a price that makes sense"
- "without the agency overhead"
- "we'll take care of it"
- "you focus on your business"
- "built for small businesses, not enterprise"

### Words We Avoid

- **Synergy, leverage, ecosystem, holistic, seamless** — agency buzzwords that mean nothing
- **Solutions, deliverables, stakeholders** — corporate speak
- **Cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, innovative** — every agency says this
- **Empower, transform, revolutionize** — overpromising SaaS language
- **Best-in-class, world-class** — means nothing without proof
- Technical jargon without explanation: Core Web Vitals, schema markup, CWV, CTR (either explain it or cut it)

### Signature Phrases

- **Tagline:** "Built for small businesses. Not enterprise." — protect this at all costs. It's the best line on the site.
- **Promise:** "We'll take care of it — you focus on your business."
- **Linguistic pattern:** Short declarative sentences. Two or three words. Then the payoff. Like this.

## Writing Guidelines

### Do's
- Use contractions freely — *"we're"*, *"you'll"*, *"it's"*, *"don't"*
- Lead with the client's problem, not your service feature
- Name the enemy (overpriced agencies, confusing contracts) when relevant — it earns trust
- Use second person: *"you"* and *"your business"*, not *"clients"* or *"customers"*
- Short sentences. Then a longer one to give it rhythm and breathing room.
- Explain every technical term or remove it entirely
- Let prices be visible — transparency is a brand value, not a risk
- Write headlines that a plumber in Ohio would understand in 3 seconds

### Don'ts
- Don't start a sentence with *"We"* — it shifts focus away from the client
- Don't use passive voice in CTAs — *"Get Your Quote"* not *"A Quote Can Be Requested"*
- Don't stack adjectives — *"professional, affordable, fast"* means nothing; pick one and prove it
- Don't write long paragraphs on service pages — use bullets or short punchy sentences
- Don't use stock agency phrases: *"full-service digital agency"*, *"data-driven"*, *"360-degree approach"*
- Don't over-explain your process — clients care about the outcome, not the workflow
- Don't be falsely modest (avoid *"we think"*, *"we believe"*, *"we try to"*) — own your claims

## Brand Messaging Hierarchy

### Tagline
**"Built for small businesses. Not enterprise."**
Assessment: Perfect. Already written. Do not change it. This is the brand's Rebel signal in 7 words.

### Value Propositions
1. Your website delivered in 14 days, no waiting, no back-and-forth
2. Flat-rate pricing, you know the cost before we start
3. No contracts on the one-time build; optional month-to-month retainers with no lock-in for ongoing SEO
4. SEO included, not an add-on you have to ask for

Note: earlier drafts of this pitch said "no retainers" as an absolute. CopperBuilds now offers optional monthly retainer plans (Local Presence, Lead Machine, Market Leader per `pricing.html`), cancel anytime with 30 days notice. The differentiator is no lock-in contracts, not the absence of retainers as a service.

### Elevator Pitch (75 words)
"Most web agencies are built for enterprise clients. Their prices, timelines, and contracts prove it. CopperBuilds is built for the other 99%, small businesses that need a real website, fast, without the overhead. We build mobile-first, SEO-ready sites in 14 days at flat rates. No surprise invoices, no lock-in contracts, no jargon. Just a website that works, from a team that's actually on your side."

### Boilerplate (120 words)
"CopperBuilds is a web design studio built for small businesses, serving home services pros across the US. We build fast, mobile-first websites with SEO included, and we deliver them in 14 days at flat-rate pricing with no contracts.

Most small businesses get the short end of the agency deal: long timelines, confusing contracts, and invoices that grow. We built CopperBuilds to fix that. Every service we offer is designed to help local businesses get found online and turn visitors into real customers, at a price that actually makes sense.

We handle the website. You focus on your business. That's the whole deal."

### Full Brand Story
Current status: Missing. Recommend writing a 300-word founder story for the About page that explains *why* CopperBuilds exists, the specific moment or frustration that created it. This is the Rebel origin story and it's the most trust-building content a small service business can have.

**Differentiation Assessment:**

- CopperBuilds' natural differentiation: the only agency that actually sounds like it was built by someone who cares about small businesses, not just a vendor trying to close a deal.

## Recommendations

### Immediate Actions (status as of the rebrand)
1. **Protect the tagline** — "Built for small businesses. Not enterprise." must appear on every page, especially the homepage hero. Still active guidance.
2. **Rewrite the homepage headline** — still worth checking against the live site; the copy samples above have options.
3. **Simplify service descriptions** — remove all unexplained jargon (Core Web Vitals, schema markup, GSC). Replace with plain outcomes. Still active guidance.
4. **Add the founder's origin story** — the About page could use a human, honest paragraph about *why* CopperBuilds exists. Still active guidance if not yet written.

### Voice Evolution Opportunities
1. **Add a Rebel statement to the homepage** — one honest line that calls out the agency industry problem. Example: "Most agencies charge enterprise prices for small business work. We don't."
2. **Make pricing language louder** — "flat-rate" and "no contracts" should be front and center, not buried.
3. **Write the blog in conversational voice** — teach like a friend, not a content marketing machine.

### Visual Identity Alignment — Resolved by the Rebrand
This section originally called for a new visual system to match the voice (warm palette, approachable typography, removal of dark-mode/SaaS anti-patterns). That work is done: the CopperBuilds identity now lives in `DESIGN.md` and matches every point originally listed here. No further action needed on this item; `DESIGN.md` is the source of truth going forward.
